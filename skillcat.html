SkillCat is a mobile training app for HVAC, electrical and plumbing. Learners earn the certifications that get them hired, and employers use it to train their teams.

The Brief

Learners were signing up, then stalling before their first certificate, and working pros had little reason to come back. The goal was one clear path from sign-up to first cert to first job.

Business goals

  • ActivationFree lesson within 7 days
  • ConversionFree → certificate → Premium
  • B2B growthCompany manager dashboard

Design goals

  • One pathSign-up → first cert → first job
  • CredibilityCertifications visible at decision points
  • One systemWorks for beginners, pros and managers

Problem statement

  • LearnersUnsure which course to start as an aspiring technician
  • ProsWorking techs have little reason to come back after being certified
  • UnknownWhich barrier matters most for each audience?

Competitor Audit

I audited 7 trade schools and learning apps. Trade schools felt credible but dated; consumer apps felt modern but casual. None were both, and that's the gap SkillCat could fill.

Direct, trade schools & cert providers: logos, type, palettes, and product screens.

Indirect, consumer learning apps: logos, type, palettes, and product screens.

Target Users

I designed for aspiring tradespeople first and working technicians second. Managers and team leads were out of scope for the learner app.

Prioritization

I ranked every opportunity by impact and effort. Quick wins like certifications up front came first; the social feed and simulations tab were cut.

Quick wins

  • Certifications up front
  • Certs one tap away
  • Clear next steps
  • Personalized onboarding

Big bets

  • Job placement tools
  • Proctored exams in-app
  • Job finding tools

Fill-ins

  • Study reminders
  • Resume builder
  • Portfolio builder

Cut

  • Social media
  • Simulations tab
  • Practice exams

Reflection

01

Decide on evidence, and say how sure you are

The texting study was small, so I treated what I heard as a strong signal, not proof. When I made a call, I named what it rested on and how confident I was, so the team knew which decisions were settled and which were worth revisiting.

02

A design is never done

Several times we thought we had the final iteration, then testing with more users surfaced new ways to improve. I learned to treat iteration as continuous and never settle on a design as finished.

03

Designing and coding at the same time

Prototyping in code while designing often showed me a screen was too complex to build, or that transitional screens were missing entirely, since our visual designer focused on the core screens. Raising these with the team before engineering handoff headed off problems and sped up the product lifecycle.
