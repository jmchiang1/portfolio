01 · The condition

Sleep apnea

Breathing stops and starts through the night, sometimes hundreds of times. Usually the airway collapses (obstructive); less often the brain misses a beat (central).

02 · The treatment

CPAP

A bedside machine pushes steady air through a mask to hold the airway open, and logs every breath to an SD card. Whether the mask fits decides whether people keep wearing it.

User Interviews

I talked to six CPAP users about what gets in their way.

What I heard, again and again

Mask fit is the real battle

Nearly everyone cycled through many masks (Kathy tried nine) before one stuck — and discomfort is exactly where people quit.

Apps are all-or-nothing

myAir felt too shallow; OSCAR too complex. Everyone wanted the missing middle — real detail, without the overwhelm.

They want a guide, not a dashboard

From “virtual assistant” to ChatGPT consults, users already reach for AI to interpret the data and say what to try next.

It feels clinical — it should feel human

Compliance pressure makes therapy feel like being a medical device. People wanted warmth, autonomy, and encouragement.

02 The Problem

Most people quit CPAP therapy before it can help them.

The problem

CPAP apps either show raw charts or hide everything behind one score. Either way, people can’t tell what went wrong last night or what to change.

What Nocta does

Nocta reads each night from the machine’s SD card and tells you what happened, what likely caused it, and one thing to try tonight.

Competitive Analysis

CPAP apps show the data without explaining it, and sleep apps that explain well ignore CPAP.

04 The Result

Here’s what Nocta looks like today.

01

The why horizon

Instead of a wall of charts, the first thing you see is a verdict and the shape of your night: a few words on what happened, one line with a reference point, and a curve of events per hour against your usual.

Tap the info icon and Nox shows its work: the exact numbers it reasoned from, whether the night fits a pattern it's watching, and how confident it is.

02

The full night, one chart at a time

The detail layer: events by type on the night's real clock, then every signal the machine records, from breathing rate and pressure to leak, flow limitation and snore.

Tap any chart for its own page, like Apple Health: the key number, the graph at full size, and a plain-language “About.”

03

Ask Nox

Nox opens already knowing what you were looking at. From the horizon it starts with last night's numbers and offers three follow-ups; from Trends or a chart, it starts there instead.

Its limits are part of the design. Ask it to change your pressure and it says that's your doctor's call, then offers to prepare a summary for them.

04

Trends over time

Zoom out to 7, 30 or 90 nights. Four tiles (AHI, leak, usage and pressure) double as tabs for one large chart, each with its change right beside the number.

Week-level insights, a best-versus-worst night comparison and journal patterns connect what you logged to how you slept.

05 How Insights Are Made

How Nocta decides what to suggest each morning.

insights.js · simplified

function morningCard(date) {
  const night = cardNight(date);

Last night’s data, read from your SD card.

  const ctx = {
    usual: baselineFor(date),

Your normal: the middle of your last 30 nights.

    setup: getAnswers(),

Your mask type and sleep position, from setup.

    checkin: checkinTags(date),

This morning’s check-in answers.

    quiet: quietKinds(date),

How it learns: rules you said were wrong rest for 2 weeks.

  };
  const found = DETECTORS
    .map((rule) => rule(night, ctx))

21 rules, each looking for one thing, like a leak.

    .filter((f) => f && !isQuiet(ctx, f.kind))

Skips those rules, unless it’s something for your doctor.

    .sort((a, b) => b.priority - a.priority);

The most important finding goes first.

  return write(found[0], ctx);
}

06 Beta Findings

Testers worried about who could read their health data, so I locked it down.

About 175 people tried the beta and 24 imported their own SD card. Within five days of their feedback, every place Nocta keeps their data was encrypted.

Your computer

Reads the SD card in your browser

HTTPS

Nocta’s server

Locks each night with AES-256

Locked

Storage

Keeps only locked copies

Oura and Whoop keys are encrypted and never reach the browser.

Research copies have no name attached and are encrypted at rest.

Passwords need upper, lower, a number and a symbol.

A written breach plan, with keys that can be swapped safely.

07 What I Learned

What I learned from shipping Nocta to real users.

The safest AI is predictable

Rules can be read, tested and published. That mattered more than fluent prose.

Real data breaks the happy path

A real card brought 20-minute nights and nights in pieces. Each one needed a calm state.

Voice is a system too

Hedged causes, one action, no diagnosis: enforced in every screen and every Nox answer.

Privacy is part of the design

Session replays mask every number and chart, and shared nights are anonymised.
