CourtFit · AI Facility Underwriting

Turning any listing into a go / no-go in seconds.

CourtFit is an AI-assisted underwriting tool for indoor badminton & pickleball facilities. Paste a commercial listing and it sizes the courts, models the build-out and returns, scores local demand against the competition, and flags the NYC / Nassau code work — collapsing a week of broker calls and spreadsheets into one screen.

Overview

Opening an indoor badminton or pickleball facility starts with a brutal real-estate filter: of the commercial listings on the market, which warehouse or flex space can actually become a profitable court house? Answering that means juggling ceiling height, square footage, parking, zoning, build-out cost, neighborhood demand, and the existing competition — scattered across listing PDFs, the Census, Google Maps, and a back-of-napkin pro-forma.

CourtFit folds all of it into one tool. The four flows below trace the real path an operator takes: add a listing, read its verdict, place it on the map against demand and competition, then compare the shortlist to pick the winner.

01 · Capture

Paste a listing, skip the data entry.

A LoopNet listing → structured deal in one click.

The flow

A broker's listing is unstructured prose and a photo gallery. CourtFit ships a bookmarklet that grabs the page straight from LoopNet (which blocks server-side scrapers), hands it to the app, and a language model extracts the hard facts — square footage, clear height, rent, zoning, parking — plus the photos. No form to fill out.

The moment it's added, the calculator turns usable area into a court layout and a full pro-forma, so a raw listing becomes a rated deal before you've finished reading it.

02 · The verdict

Everything to decide, on one screen.

Open a property to see the full underwrite.

The flow

Clicking a property opens its verdict: the rating and headline numbers up top — court count, NOI, payback — then the detail. A vision pass over the listing photos scales the renovation estimate (a bare shell trends toward a full gut), a code panel flags the NYC / Nassau checklist a sports facility hits, and the trade-area demographics score local badminton and pickleball demand.

The design problem here was trust: deciding where the model gives a confident number and where it honestly says “verify this.”

Deal math

Court layout, revenue, NOI, payback, and a rating from “Strong Candidate” to “Do Not Pursue.”

Condition & code

Vision-scaled renovation cost plus the assembly-occupancy / zoning / parking due-diligence checklist.

Demand & whitespace

Census demand by sport, discounted by nearby competitors — unmet demand, not just population.

03 · Pipeline map

Your pipeline and the competition, mapped.

Sites, competitors, and demand on one canvas.

The flow

The map layers your candidate sites against existing badminton and pickleball facilities. Toggle My sites and Competitors, filter by sport / borough / court count, and click any marker for a quick read on its demand and nearby competition.

Flip on the Demand heatmap and roughly 2,500 Census tracts across the five boroughs and Nassau light up by population — the greenfield finder for underserved pockets worth scouting before a listing even exists.

Layers & filters

My sites vs. competitors, by sport, region, and court count — with a live trade-area ring.

Click for detail

Any pin opens its demand + competition snapshot; jump straight to the full verdict.

Demand heatmap

~2,500 tracts rendered as a live heat field to surface where to open next.

04 · Ranking

Which deal wins?

A weighted leaderboard that turns a folder of maybes into a shortlist.

The flow

The Compare tab normalizes every saved candidate across six dimensions — whitespace, profitability, payback speed, court capacity, build-readiness, and code safety — relative to the rest of your set, then blends them into one composite score.

Sliders let you weight what matters for a given deal: a cash buyer cranks profitability and payback, an operator chasing a flagship leans on whitespace and capacity. The leaderboard re-ranks live, so “which one should I pursue?” becomes a tunable, defensible answer.

Reflection

CourtFit was a test of how far a single designer can take a real product with AI in the loop — not just generating code, but doing the work inside the app: reading listings, judging a space's condition from photos, and reasoning about building code. The interesting design problem wasn't the pro-forma; it was deciding where the model should give a confident number and where it should honestly say “verify this,” so the tool earns trust instead of faking certainty.

It also reframed “best location” for me. Demand alone is a vanity metric — the real signal is demand the market hasn't captured yet. Building the whitespace score and the heatmap turned a fuzzy instinct (“Flushing feels right”) into something measurable, and made the case-study question — best facility in the best place — answerable on one screen.
